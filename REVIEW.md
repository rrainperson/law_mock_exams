## 2. Source hierarchy applied

1. **Taught course material** (lecture and seminar content, worked problems, the sample multiple-choice
   items and the decided cases discussed in class) — the primary basis for every new question.
2. **Prescribed textbooks** — *Constitutions Compared* (6th edn) for International Law and
   *Contract Law: A Comparative Introduction* (3rd edn) for Private Law — used only to confirm
   terminology, resolve ambiguity in the taught material and fill genuine gaps.
3. Nothing was added merely because it appears in a textbook but was not taught.

Copyright and privacy: all questions are freshly drafted paraphrase. No extended quotation from either
textbook is reproduced. The personal watermark present on the supplied copy of the constitutional-law
textbook appears nowhere in any deliverable.

## 4. Changes made to the carried-over Introduction to Law questions

The instruction was to reuse these questions rather than replace them. Their substance is unchanged.
Three categories of minimal edit were applied so that they satisfy the rule that a question must not
depend on the order in which a student takes the classes:

1. **Source labels remapped (all 312).** The displayed source is now `Introduction to Law — <topic>`.
   The original label is retained in the non-displayed `legacySource` field for traceability.
2. **23 stems de-referenced.** Phrases such as "described in the notes", "according to the workshop"
   and "in the workshop materials" were removed, leaving the substantive question intact.
   Affected questions: 43, 45, 64, 77, 135, 140, 181, 185, 217, 221, 222, 234, 235, 254, 260, 266,
   273, 285, 297, 300, 304, 305, 307.
3. **15 explanations and 3 options rephrased.** Explanations that began "Workshop 6 directs…" or
   "The slides identify…" now state the rule directly. Three options describing types of secondary
   source were made generic ("an academic commentary article", "a teaching handout",
   "a student's summary of a class session").

Questions 234 and 235 concern a fictional false-imprisonment rule used for analysis practice. They
were retained because the reasoning skill is transferable, and the stems now describe the rule as
fictional rather than attributing it to a particular class.

## 6. Distractor design

Every newly written question contains at least one distractor that is a deliberate near-miss of the
key. Recurring near-miss pairs used:

* disapplication vs annulment · ex ante vs abstract · concrete vs abstract · centralised vs decentralised
* parliament vs legislature · head of state vs head of government · impeachment vs vote of no confidence
* absolute vs suspensive veto · mandatory vs binding referendum · federal vs devolved vs decentralised
* monism vs dualism · ratification vs transposition · signature vs entry into force
* offer vs invitation to treat · counter-offer vs request for information · rejection vs lapse
* dispatch vs receipt · sufficient vs adequate · past consideration vs executed consideration
* void vs voidable vs unenforceable · procedural vs substantive fairness
* primary vs secondary source · default vs mandatory rule · notarial deed vs writing vs written evidence

Questions testing a pure definition were kept to a minimum; most items require applying a distinction
to short facts, which is the format used in the modules.
